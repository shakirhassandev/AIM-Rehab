Hearing that your child needs a leg brace can bring up a lot of questions. Will it hurt? Will other kids notice? How will we manage shoes?

These worries are completely normal. The good news is that most children get used to their braces much faster than their parents expect. Here's what you should know.

## Why do children wear leg braces?

A leg brace (often an **AFO**, which stands for ankle foot orthosis) holds the foot and ankle in a better position. This can help your child:

- Stand with better balance
- Walk with fewer trips and falls
- Stop walking on their toes
- Keep tight muscles stretched
- Use less energy when they walk

Children often wear braces if they have **cerebral palsy**, **spina bifida**, a muscle condition, low muscle tone, or **toe walking** that hasn't stopped on its own. Some children wear them for a short time. Others wear them for years, with a new pair as they grow.

## Types of braces your child might need

- **AFO.** Goes from under the foot up the back of the calf. The most common type.
- **SMO.** A smaller brace that sits just above the ankle bones. It helps with flat or rolling feet.
- **KAFO.** A longer brace that also supports the knee.
- **Night splint.** Worn while sleeping to gently stretch tight muscles.

Your child's doctor, physiotherapist and orthotist will work out which one fits their needs.

## What happens at the fitting?

We try to make appointments relaxed and even fun.

1. **We chat with you** about your child's health, therapy and daily life.
2. **We watch your child move.** We'd rather see them play and walk than sit still on a bed.
3. **We take a cast.** Plaster bandage is wrapped around the foot and leg. It feels warm and a bit damp. It doesn't hurt, and it's quick.
4. **We make the brace** from that cast.
5. **Fitting day.** Your child tries the brace with their shoes. We check for pressure and make small changes.

Tip: bring a favorite toy, tablet or snack. A calm child makes casting quicker and easier for everyone.

## Choosing shoes that work with braces

Shoes are often the biggest headache for parents. Look for shoes that:

- **Open wide**, with laces or Velcro that go far down the front
- Have a **removable insole**, so there's more room inside
- Are a **size or width bigger** than your child's usual size
- Have a **firm heel** and a flat, grippy sole

Bring a pair to your fitting and we'll check them together.

## Helping your child get used to their brace

- **Start slowly.** An hour or two on day one, then add time each day.
- **Use the right socks.** Thin, smooth socks that come higher than the top of the brace.
- **Check their skin** each time the brace comes off. Red marks should fade within about 30 minutes.
- **Make it routine.** Put the brace on at the same times each day.
- **Let them have a say.** Children who feel involved are more likely to wear their brace.
- **Talk to school.** Share the wearing plan with your child's teacher.

## How often do children need new braces?

Children grow quickly, so most need a new brace about once a year. Some need one sooner during a growth spurt.

Signs your child may have outgrown their brace:

- Toes hang over the end of the footplate
- New red marks that don't fade
- The straps barely close
- Your child suddenly refuses to wear it

If you notice any of these, book a check. Don't wait for the next planned visit.
