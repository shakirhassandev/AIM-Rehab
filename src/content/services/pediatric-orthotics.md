## Why children need a different approach

Children aren't small adults. Their bones are still growing, their muscles are changing and they're learning new skills all the time. A brace that fits perfectly today may be too small in a few months.

So we plan ahead. We check your child's braces often, and we watch how they move as they grow. We also take time to explain things to you as a parent, because you're the one helping with the brace every day.

## Conditions we often see

We work with children who have:

- **Cerebral palsy**, where muscles can be tight or weak
- **Toe walking** that carries on past the toddler years
- **Flat feet** that cause pain or tiredness
- **Spina bifida** and other spinal conditions
- **Muscle conditions** such as muscular dystrophy
- **Clubfoot**, as part of the treatment plan from your child's doctor
- Delayed walking or balance problems

If you're worried about how your child walks or stands, we're happy to take a look and explain what we see.

## Braces and devices for children

### AFOs and SMOs

An **AFO** (ankle foot orthosis) supports the foot and ankle and goes up the back of the calf. An **SMO** (supramalleolar orthosis) is a smaller brace that sits just above the ankle bones. Both help keep the foot in a better position for standing and walking. Read our parent guide on [leg braces for children](/blog/leg-braces-for-children/) to learn more.

### KAFOs and night splints

Some children need support at the knee too, which is where a KAFO helps. Others wear a splint at night to gently stretch tight muscles while they sleep.

### Insoles

For children with painful flat feet or other foot problems, custom insoles can make standing and walking more comfortable. See our page on [custom foot orthotics](/services/custom-foot-orthotics/).

### Walkers and mobility support

Some children need extra help to stand or move around. We can give advice on walkers, standing support and seating, and how braces work together with them.

## What happens at your child's appointment

1. **We talk with you.** We ask about your child's health, therapy and daily routine.
2. **We watch your child move.** Through play, walking and standing, not just on the exam bed.
3. **We take a cast or measurements.** We keep it quick and explain each step to your child.
4. **Fitting.** Your child tries the brace with their shoes and we adjust it.
5. **Growth checks.** We see your child again every few months to check the fit.

## Tips for parents

- Build up wearing time slowly over the first week or two.
- Check your child's skin after each wear. Red marks should fade within about 30 minutes.
- Use thin, smooth socks that come higher than the brace.
- Make it part of the routine, like brushing teeth.
- Tell us if your child suddenly doesn't want to wear it. There's often a fit reason.
